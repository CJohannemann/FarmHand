One

A cow, a flock and a bed of tomatoes don't keep the same kind of record.

An animal is tracked by name and tag — sex, birth date, breed, the works. A flock is tracked by headcount instead, because nobody names bird eleven of forty. A planting belongs to a bed, for one season. Land, equipment and stores — feed, seed, meat, produce — all live in the same record, each carrying what actually describes it rather than being forced into one shape.

On the farm behind this screen, that's two cattle, one hen, a tomato planting, and fifty pounds of layer feed on hand — and Farm Hand Manager didn't need to know any of that in advance to have somewhere to put it.

Two

Buy something. Feed something. Write down what happened.

Today opens on a short row of tiles — Eggs, Milk, Pick, Feed, Buy, Note, Plan — and only shows the ones that apply to what's actually on the farm. No eggs tile until there are birds. Each one is a couple of taps, entered in the barn with no signal; it syncs itself once you're back in range.

Three

Know what it's actually costing you — down to the animal.

Every purchase and every animal carries its cost forward automatically. Analytics adds it up by week, month, quarter or year, broken out by category — cost-per-pound as a lookup, not a project someone has to sit down and do.
